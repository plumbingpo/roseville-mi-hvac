How Ann Arbor Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

Routine maintenance is the difference between a system that runs for years and one that quits in the middle of the season. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a new filter every one to three months, at least two feet of open space around the outdoor condenser, a gentle rinse each spring for the condenser coil, and two professional tune-ups a year, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular service, central air usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts acting up, like higher energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and causes far less stress than an emergency swap during the first heat wave of summer.

If comfort problems do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A weak capacitor is a quick fix. The same capacitor ignored for a season can take the compressor with it and turn a minor repair into a full system replacement. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and sleepless nights.

Typical Heating & Cooling Issues in Ann Arbor

HVAC systems in this area face a familiar set of problems. Long cooling seasons push compressors and capacitors to their limits, and salt air and moisture chew at outdoor condenser coils and fins, cutting efficiency years before the equipment should show its age. Clogged filters quietly starve systems of airflow, freezing evaporator coils in summer and overheating furnace heat exchangers in winter, a slow kind of damage most homeowners never see coming.

We also see the full range of component failures in local homes: capacitors that leave the outdoor unit humming but not starting, contactors with pitted points, cracked igniters and dirty flame sensors that lock furnaces out on cold mornings, refrigerant leaks that turn into long lukewarm afternoons, and blower motors worn down by years of restricted airflow. The area's older housing stock adds its own layer, undersized returns, leaky or crushed ductwork, and additions that no duct run ever reached.

Every one of these problems is fixable, and most are much less expensive when caught early. If your system is doing anything unusual, strange noises, weak airflow, rooms that won't hold temperature, bills creeping upward, a quick diagnostic visit from our team catches the small problem before it becomes an expensive one.
